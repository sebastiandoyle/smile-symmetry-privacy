Smile Symmetry Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 September 2026 · Terms of Use · Support

In short: Smile Symmetry uses your front camera to measure how evenly you smile, live, entirely on your iPhone. Camera frames and face measurements are processed in memory and never stored, sent to us or shared. Only your best score and, if you save one, a smile snapshot of three numbers are kept on the device. Purchases go through Apple. There are no third-party SDKs, analytics, ads or accounts.

1. Who is responsible for your data

Smile Symmetry is published by Sebastian Doyle, an individual developer. For the UK GDPR and the EU GDPR, Sebastian Doyle is the data controller. For the Australian Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), Sebastian Doyle is the entity that handles your personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs).

Contact: sebastian.doyle@outlook.com

5. What we do not do

  • We do not store or upload photos, video, depth data or any representation of your face.
  • We do not use face data to identify or verify you, and we do not create face templates.
  • We do not run advertising or ad networks.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • We do not sell or rent personal information.
  • We do not use analytics or any third-party SDK.
  • We do not ask you to create an account.

6. Permissions and consent

  • Camera: to see your face and measure your smile in real time. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.

You can withdraw a permission at any time in iOS Settings > Smile Symmetry. The feature that needs it will stop working; the rest of the app will keep working.

7. Lawful bases (UK and EU GDPR)

  • Contract: processing a purchase and restoring it, so you get what you paid for.
  • Legitimate interests: validating receipts and preventing fraud or abuse of purchases. You can object (see section 10).
  • Consent: access to device features such as the camera, microphone, location, photos, motion or notifications, given through the iOS permission prompt.

8. How long data is kept

Data on your device stays there until you delete it in the app or delete the app. If your device backs up to iCloud, the app's data may be in that backup, under Apple's control and your iCloud settings.

9. International transfers

We do not transfer your personal data anywhere, because we do not receive it.

Apple may process data in the United States and other countries under its own privacy policy and its transfer safeguards.

12. Children

Smile Symmetry is rated 4+ and is suitable for general audiences, but it is not directed at children. It does not collect personal information from anyone, including children: camera frames and face measurements stay in memory on the device. Purchases are made through the Apple Account holder, who can use Apple's parental controls (Ask to Buy, Screen Time).

13. Security

On-device data is protected by iOS data protection and your device passcode. Data sent to processors travels over encrypted (HTTPS/TLS) connections.

Face data and biometrics

Where it goes: nowhere. Face data (camera frames, ARKit face-tracking values and Vision landmark points) is processed only on your device, in memory, while the training screen is open. It is never written to storage, never sent to us, Apple or any third party, and is discarded frame by frame. The only thing kept is a score of three numbers (left, right, symmetry) and a date.

Is it biometric or special-category data? Under the UK and EU GDPR, facial data is special-category “biometric data” only when it is processed to uniquely identify a person (Article 9). Smile Symmetry never uses face data to identify or verify anyone, creates no face template, and we never receive it, so we do not process special-category biometric data. Under the Australian Privacy Act, biometric information is sensitive information when used for automated verification or identification, or as a biometric template; the app does neither. The data is still personal information about your face, so we treat it with the same care: on-device only, used only for your live score, and only with your camera permission.

Apple's ARKit face tracking does not give apps your Face ID data, and the app does not access Face ID.
